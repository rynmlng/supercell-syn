## Confidence & Caveats

### Confidence: MODERATE-LOW for supercell mode
- **High confidence:** Strong discrete thunderstorms will fire along the dry line in SE Nebraska during the afternoon and expand into Kansas by evening
- **Moderate confidence:** Cells will reach 45–60 dBZ (severe-caliber reflectivity)
- **Low confidence:** Sustained supercell mode. All three soundings show marginal-to-poor supercell composite parameters. The best shear profile (north-central KS) is under a strong cap. The best CAPE/no-cap environment (SE NE) has negative SRH and weak bulk shear.
- **Storm mode likely:** Pulse-type multicells or short-lived supercells. High CAPE will support large hail and strong outflow winds even without sustained rotation.
- **Upside scenario:** If the cap breaks in central Kansas where the shear is better (0-3km bulk shear 25 kt, BRN shear 38), more organized supercells could fire along the dry line in the late afternoon.

### Key Risks
1. Weak low-level shear may prevent supercell sustenance
2. The cap in Kansas may hold longer than expected, delaying initiation
3. Multiple cells could cluster quickly, reducing isolation
4. This is a typical July 4th pattern: high CAPE, marginal shear, favoring strong multicells over classic supercells

## Summary
The July 4th, 2026 setup features a well-defined dry line through central Kansas with extreme instability (CAPE 2000-3000+ J/kg) but marginal deep-layer shear. The HRRR shows discrete cells initiating along the dry line in SE Nebraska by early afternoon (fh=7-9, 2-4 PM CDT), intensifying to 45 dBZ, then expanding explosively into Kansas by evening (55-60 dBZ by fh=12). The SPC has Enhanced risk over central Kansas. The best chase strategy is to position in western Missouri to intercept SE Nebraska cells as they propagate SSE into the Enhanced risk corridor. Storm mode will likely be pulse multicell to marginal supercell, with the primary threats being large hail and damaging outflow winds from high-CAPE storms.
